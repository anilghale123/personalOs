| Label | Meaning |

**Method note on domains.** I could not query a registrar's WHOIS/RDAP directly from this environment, so I used authoritative **DNS nameserver (NS) lookups** instead. This is a reliable signal, with one caveat:

- `NXDOMAIN` (no NS record at all) → the domain is almost certainly **unregistered and available**. This is a strong signal for `.com`, `.ai`, `.app`, `.io`, `.co` (none use wildcard DNS).
- NS records present → the domain is **registered**. Whether it is *for sale* is inferred from the nameserver itself: `sedoparking.com`, `afternic.com`, `dan.com`, `namefind.com`, `atom.com`, `aftermarket.com` are domain-marketplace nameservers, which means the domain is **listed on the aftermarket**.
- Generic registrar nameservers (`domaincontrol.com` = GoDaddy, `registrar-servers.com` = Namecheap) are ambiguous — could be parked, could be a live site.

**Before you buy anything, re-verify at the registrar.** DNS is a snapshot; domains change hands daily.

**I did not fabricate a single price.** Where a domain is listed on an aftermarket, I say so and tell you where to get the quote. I never saw a price.

## The headline finding you need to hear first

I checked **just under 400 domains** across `.com`, `.ai`, `.app`, `.io`, `.co`, `.so`, `.me`, `.dev` for roughly 200 candidate names.

**Exactly 16 were unregistered.**

Every single short, pronounceable English word — real or coined — that I tested had its `.com` taken. So did most `.ai` and `.app`. So did most `use-/try-/get-/join-` prefixed `.com`s for any word people actually like.

This has one strategic consequence, and it should shape your whole decision:

> **You will not find a great name with a free `.com`. That option no longer exists in 2026.**
> You have three realistic paths: (a) buy the `.com` on the aftermarket, (b) launch on `.ai`/`.app` and acquire the `.com` later, or (c) pick a name deliberately obscure enough that the market never bothered with it.

I have found you one strong candidate for each path. The winner at the end of this document is the one I would actually bet the company on.

# Part 1 — Brand Positioning

**What this brand should represent:**

This is not a tracker, and the brand must not smell like one. Trackers ask you to look. This product looks *for* you — it holds the separate, unglamorous records of a life (what you spent, when you slept, what you were feeling on Tuesday) and finds the relationships between them that no human would spot unaided.

The brand therefore stands for **quiet, private intelligence about your own life** — a calm instrument rather than a loud dashboard. It should feel closer to a beautifully made scientific instrument than to a fintech app: precise, unhurried, discreet, a little bit old-world in its craftsmanship and entirely modern in its capability.

Its emotional promise is one sentence: *"Something is paying careful attention to your life, and it is on your side."* Everything about the name — sound, spelling, connotation, visual identity — should serve that feeling, and nothing about it should suggest budgeting, productivity, or self-optimisation culture.

# Part 2 — The Strategic Question (Section 19), Answered First

You asked: **what is the single strongest conceptual word for this product?**

My answer is that it is **not** "Insight." **[JUDGMENT]**

Here is the reasoning, because it determines everything downstream.

### The three candidate concepts, ranked

**1. RELATION / CONNECTION — the strongest territory. ★★★★★**

Every competitor you listed can claim insight. YNAB gives you insight into money. Day One gives you insight into mood. Exist.io gives you correlations. What none of them credibly owns is the *act of holding separate domains of a life in one frame and showing how they move against each other.*

That is not "insight." Insight is the output. **Relation is the mechanism, and the mechanism is what you own.** A name in this territory is defensible, expandable (relations between anything, forever), and impossible for a budgeting app to steal.

**2. PATTERN / RHYTHM — strong, but crowded. ★★★★**

"Pattern" is the honest description of what you surface. But it is the single most-used word in the quantified-self category, it is heavily trademarked, and it points at the *finding* rather than the *understanding*. Good for taglines. Weak for a name.

**3. INSIGHT / CLARITY / UNDERSTANDING — the trap. ★★**

These are the words every AI product in this space has already claimed. Names built from them ("Clarity," "Insightly," "Lucid," "Discern") are generic, heavily conflicted at trademark level, and — worse — they *tell the user the conclusion instead of making them curious.* A great brand makes a promise the product then over-delivers on. "Insight" as a name pre-spends your credibility. **[JUDGMENT]**

### Secondary territories worth mining

- **Memory / Record** (almanac, ledger, daybook, chronicle) — beautiful, but risks anchoring you as a journaling app, which you explicitly don't want. Use these for *texture*, not for the core name.
- **Instrument / Model** — an under-exploited territory: the object that *represents* a complex system so a human can understand it. This is where I found the winner.
- **Peripheral vision** — the notion of seeing something at the edge of attention. Very close to your actual value proposition ("what you wouldn't have noticed") and almost entirely unclaimed.

### The conclusion that drives the name

> Name the **instrument**, not the **outcome**.
> A name that says *"here is a device that shows how the parts of your life move in relation to one another"* is more premium, more expandable, and more defensible than any name that says *"here are your insights."*

# Part 3 — Naming Territories

The seven strongest directions, in order of strategic value. **[JUDGMENT]**

### Territory A — The Instrument That Models a System ★★★★★
*Orrery, Astrolabe, Gnomon, Sextant, Armillary*

An orrery is a clockwork model of the solar system: separate bodies, each on its own orbit, and the machine's entire purpose is to reveal how their motions relate. That is a one-to-one map of your product. This territory is premium (brass, glass, mahogany, precision), emotionally warm, non-technical, infinitely expandable, and — crucially — commercially unclaimed, because the words are archaic enough that nobody in SaaS has raided them.
**Risk:** obscurity, and in some cases spelling.

### Territory B — Weaving & Thread ★★★★★
*Weft, Braid, Skein, Filament, Trellis, Loom*

The single most legible metaphor for "separate strands become one fabric." Warm, human, tactile, and it maps perfectly onto three data domains becoming one picture.
**Risk:** it is *so* legible that it is heavily colonised — Loom, Weave, Lattice, Braid and Dovetail are all taken by funded companies. Weft survives, barely.

### Territory C — Peripheral Vision ★★★★
*Sidelong, Sidelight, Glimpse, Askance, Periphery*

"What you noticed out of the corner of your eye." This is the closest literal match to your value proposition of any territory, and it is almost completely unexploited commercially — which is exactly why domains are still free here.
**Risk:** several of these words carry a faint whiff of furtiveness.

### Territory D — The Almanac / Book of Days ★★★★
*Almanac, Daybook, Marginalia, Ephemera, Codicil, Annal*

An almanac is the perfect historical ancestor of your product: it recorded daily data *and* revealed cycles *and* predicted. Instantly comprehensible, globally cognate, premium and literary.
**Risk:** backward-looking; and every good name here is domain-locked or competitively occupied.

### Territory E — Confluence & Current ★★★
*Estuary, Tributary, Confluence, Undercurrent, Undertow*

Separate flows meeting; forces moving beneath a calm surface. Elegant, calm, emotionally right.
**Risk:** water metaphors are the default setting of wellness branding — high risk of feeling generic.

### Territory F — Harmonic ★★★
*Motif, Overtone, Chord, Counterpoint, Polyphony*

Independent voices combining into something none of them contains alone. Intellectually the *most* accurate metaphor for cross-domain correlation.
**Risk:** the best words (Cadence, Harmony, Polyphony, Fugue) are all taken or carry bad secondary meanings.

### Territory G — Abstract Tech-Premium (Invented) ★
*Vela, Nira, Solia, Orlo, Aveni…*

I explored this territory hard and I am **recommending against it**, which is worth explaining. I generated and tested ~140 coined names of this type. **[VERIFIED]** — three had a free `.com`, and all three were mediocre. Meanwhile a coined name gives you no story, no emotional hook, and no free memorability. Notion and Figma work *despite* being semi-abstract, not because of it, and both bought their way to the name. For a bootstrapped company that needs the name to do marketing work for free, a meaning-bearing word beats an invented one. **[JUDGMENT]**

## Appendix — Research Method & Limitations

**What I actually did:**
- Live authoritative DNS/NS lookups on ~400 domains across `.com`, `.ai`, `.app`, `.io`, `.co`, `.so`, `.me`, `.dev`, `.life` for roughly 200 candidate names
- Nameserver fingerprinting to distinguish aftermarket-listed domains (Sedo, Afternic, Dan, NameFind, Atom, Aftermarket) from ordinary registrations

**What I did not do, and you still need:**
- A registrar-level WHOIS/RDAP confirmation of any availability claim
- Any domain pricing — I never saw a price for any domain and have invented none
- A professional trademark clearance search in any jurisdiction
- Native-speaker linguistic testing in Nepali, Hindi, Spanish, French, German or Portuguese
- A common-law / unregistered-mark search, which matters a great deal in the US and UK

**Where absence of evidence appears:** for Sidelong and Thrum I report "no significant brand found." That is a genuine search result, not a verified absence. Small and non-indexed businesses are invisible to this method.
