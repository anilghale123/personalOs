### Why the rejected winners fail

**Sovia** — The study eliminates eight of its own top candidates on domain grounds, then picks one it explicitly could not verify. The name is also indistinguishable from the twenty others on its own list (Veyra, Oryva, Viora, Avela, Sereva, Nivra…). Invented Latinate names in that `-ia`/`-va` mold carry no story and do no marketing work for free — the wrong trade for a bootstrapped company. Phonetically crowded: Sovos, Sovanta, Sova, Savia.

**Aperture** — *Aperture Science* is the fictional company in Portal that secretly surveils a test subject. That is a genuinely bad association for a privacy-first product handling mood and money. Add Apple's discontinued Aperture and Aperture Investors (asset management — adjacent to your finance vertical). The study recommending it is also the least reliable of the four.

**Motif / Klaris / Noda / Sift** — Best strategic framing of the set (the *guilt-driven tracking → curiosity-driven understanding* shift is worth keeping regardless of name), but the names collide hard. Sift is a large fraud-detection company. **Noda is an open-banking payments company** — direct fintech adjacency. Klaris is one letter from Apple's Claris. The study itself concedes Motif's `.com` runs six figures.

### What Orrery has that the others don't

1. **All four studies independently converged on the same positioning** — connecting separate domains of a life into one picture. Only Orrery encodes that *mechanism* literally rather than describing the *outcome*.
2. **The name contains the pitch.** "It's a clockwork model of the solar system — separate bodies, separate orbits, and the machine shows how they move in relation to each other. That's what we do with your money, sleep, habits and mood." That is a complete investor pitch, App Store description and dinner-party answer, delivered by the name.
3. **Cleanest trademark profile in the entire corpus.** No funded tech company, no finance company, no consumer app surfaced.
4. **It cannot box you in.** Orrery Finance, Orrery Signals, Orrery for Teams, Orrery API all read naturally in ten years.
5. **Free visual identity.** Concentric rings, orbital paths, brass-and-ink restraint. Your designer starts on second base.

## ⚠️ One open item — resolve before spending money

There was an **Orrery.ai** — a stealth venture from around 2017–18 that aimed to extract insights from satellite, sensor, social and purchasing data **for the financial sector**. That is uncomfortably close to your space and none of the four studies caught it.

It appears dormant: `orrery.ai` sits on NameSilo's default nameservers, which is the signature of a parked domain rather than a live product, and the founder's other venture was a space-analytics firm.

**Action:** pull the USPTO record for any ORRERY mark in Classes 9 / 36 / 42 and check whether the entity is still trading. If it's abandoned, it may even be worth asking whether they'd release the `.ai`.

## The honest cost

You will spell the name over the phone for two years. That is the trade, and it is the one thing that could reasonably change the answer.

It is survivable because *orrery* is a real dictionary word — search engines, autocorrect and App Store search all recover from "orerry" and "orary." An invented misspelling has no such safety net.

## Positioning note — keep this regardless of the name

Do **not** market the product as *"Your Personal Intelligence Platform."* That phrase is already crowded — Pi/Inflection, Mantra, Naomi, Lapsus and Overli are all occupying similar language.

Own something closer to your actual differentiation:

> **AI that connects the dots of your life.**

And keep the framing that emerged strongest across all four studies: this is a shift from **guilt-driven tracking** (budgeting and habit apps grading you) to **curiosity-driven understanding**. The brand should feel like an observant chief of staff, not an accountant.
